# Argo Journal — Learnings from Running Pop-Up Cities

*Private to Argo's concierge agents. Not shared with users directly, but used to inform concierge responses.*

## Key learnings

**1. First-timers need a buddy.** Every city edition, the people who had the best experience were paired with a returning participant on day one. The concierge should proactively suggest connections for newcomers.

**2. Logistics questions dominate the first 48 hours.** WiFi password, door code, nearest grocery store, how to get from the airport — these account for ~60% of all questions in the first two days. Have these answers at the top of the knowledge base.

**3. Shared meals are the highest-signal coordination moment.** Cities that had a structured dinner plan (rotating cooking groups, sign-up for restaurants) had much higher satisfaction scores than ones that left it to chance. The concierge should suggest dinner groups based on who's there and their dietary preferences.

**4. Interest-based introductions work better than random ones.** When we matched people who both listed "surfing" or "biohacking" or "writing," those connections lasted beyond the city. When we did random introductions, most were forgotten.

**5. Privacy matters.** Some people don't want the concierge to share their arrival time or interests. Make matchmaking opt-in, not opt-out.

**6. House rules need to be repeated.** Every 2-3 days, the concierge should gently remind about quiet hours, kitchen cleanup, and shared space etiquette. People read the rules once and forget.

**7. The "what's nearby" question never gets old.** Even on day 7, people ask what's within walking distance for dinner. Keep the neighborhood guide detailed and updated.
